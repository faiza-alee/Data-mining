Title: AI for Kids — Episode 1: What is AI?
Length: ~6:00

[0:00–0:05]
SFX: Light upbeat music fades in.
VO: Have you already used AI today?

[0:05–0:25]
VO: Maybe your phone unlocked with your face. Maybe YouTube suggested a video you liked. Maybe you asked an assistant a question. That’s AI helping in the background.

[0:25–0:35]
VO: Welcome to AI for Kids, made for classes 6 to 10. In this first episode, we’ll answer a big question: What is AI?

[0:35–1:10]
VO: Artificial Intelligence is when computers do tasks that usually need human smarts—like recognizing pictures, understanding language, or making decisions. AI doesn’t mean a robot that thinks like a human. Most AI is narrow: it learns a specific task, like sorting photos of cats and dogs.

[1:10–1:25]
VO: You’ll also hear terms like Machine Learning and Deep Learning. Here’s an easy way to remember them.

[1:25–2:00]
VO: Imagine three boxes, one inside another. The biggest box is AI—any method that makes computers act smart. Inside that is Machine Learning, where computers learn patterns from data instead of being given every rule. Inside ML is Deep Learning, which uses layered neural networks to learn complex patterns, like recognizing faces or translating languages.

[2:00–2:15]
VO: So how do machines learn? Let’s keep it simple.

[2:15–3:00]
VO: First, we collect data—like many images labeled “cat” or “dog.” The computer looks for patterns. It adjusts a model—the computer’s “recipe”—until it can guess the right label. We test the model on new images to see if it learned well. If it makes mistakes, we improve the data or the model. That’s learning from examples.

[3:00–3:20]
VO: Where do you see AI? Recommendation systems that suggest songs and videos. Assistants that answer questions. Translation apps that convert languages. Cameras that focus on faces.

[3:20–3:40]
VO: Even games use AI to control characters. And in science, AI helps doctors read scans and helps scientists discover new materials. AI is a helpful teammate.

[3:40–4:00]
VO: But we must use AI responsibly. If the data is unfair, the model can be unfair—this is called bias.

[4:00–4:20]
VO: We should protect privacy. And we should always think: Is this safe? Is it kind? AI is powerful, so we use it wisely.

[4:20–4:35]
VO: Mini Activity time! Pause and try this “Paper Classifier.”

[4:35–5:10]
VO: On five sticky notes, draw quick doodles: a sun, a cloud, a tree, a house, and a smiley face. Now make a simple rule: “If the drawing has eyes or a mouth, put it in the ‘Face’ group; else, ‘Not Face.’” Sort your notes. That’s like making a simple model.

[5:10–5:30]
VO: Improve your rules. Maybe add: “If it’s round with eyes, predict ‘Smiley’; if it has a roof shape, predict ‘House.’” You just designed features—clues that help the model predict.

[5:30–5:45]
VO: Recap: AI helps computers act smart. ML learns from data. DL learns complex patterns with layers. Use AI fairly and safely.

[5:45–6:00]
VO: Next episode, we’ll make a tiny ML project in your browser. Subscribe and bring paper and a pencil! Music out.
